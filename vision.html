ThesisWhy this exists

Science's error-correction loop runs at the speed of a human generation

Not because anyone is lazy. Because the work required is beyond what any person, or any institution, can physically do — and until about two years ago, beyond what any machine could do either.

Three failures compound, quietly, in every field at once:

i

Nothing downstream is ever recomputed

Every result rests on prior results — a stellar radius, a cross-section, a rate constant, a calibration. When one of those moves, and they move constantly, nothing that depended on it is checked. There is no mechanism, and nobody's job is to build one. Results invalidated in 2019 are cited in 2026 as if nothing happened.

ii

Forgotten is stored identically to refuted

Papers are dropped for reasons unrelated to being wrong. The author died. Wrong journal, wrong language. The field moved. It was published before the instrument existed that could test it — untestable in 1974, trivially testable now, and nobody has connected the two. The literature records neglect and refutation as the same thing: silence.

iii

The data has already outrun the reading

Rubin alone will produce millions of alerts a night. Nobody will look at most of them. The unexplained observation that changes a field is, statistically, already sitting in a public archive, and has been for years.

Parallax shortens the distance between something being wrong and someone knowing.

MechanismThe second eye

One vantage point gives you a picture. Two give you depth.

Every tool in this space reads the literature and describes it. That is one eye, and one eye cannot measure distance. Parallax has two.

The record is what you already built — 18 indices, 124 passes, one per decade back to the 1600s, so relevance ranking never buries the founding work. The sky is the live half: Rubin and ZTF alerts, TNS, GCN, LIGO/Virgo, Gaia, JPL Horizons, TESS, the Exoplanet Archive, the Planetary Data System — public, streaming, and largely unread.

Where the two disagree, there is something nobody has accounted for. That gap is not an opinion. It is a measurement, and it is the only thing Parallax is allowed to assert.

The objectWhat a claim is

Every claim is born with a figure and a way to die

Physics has no visual language for its open questions. Every unresolved problem in the universe currently looks like a grey plot with six-point axis labels. That is not an aesthetic complaint — it is why science cannot compete for attention, and why the frontier is unscrollable today.

Two rules keep this an instrument rather than a wallpaper app. The figure is computed, never illustrated — every pixel derived from the data, or it does not ship. And the figure is itself a prediction: it shows what you would see if the claim holds, which means reality can contradict the picture.

No claim ships without a stated condition that would kill it.

That single rule is the whole boundary between a provocative instrument and a forum for cranks. Speculation is welcome — about the bulk, about extra dimensions, about anything — provided it names the measurement that could end it. If nothing could disprove it, it is not a claim and it does not go on the frontier.

SurfacesWhat you use

Six surfaces, one engine

The machinery underneath — what rests on what, across the whole corpus — is never shown to anyone. Nobody sells a graph. Google is a link graph and you see a box; Git is a directed acyclic graph and you see "3 files changed". The structure is what makes the simple thing on screen possible.

TraceThe demo

Nobody has ever been able to pull the thread on a number. Tap one and it falls open, over and over, until you hit the thing everything is standing on.

On screen that whole chain is one sentence: "Your planet stopped being rocky." The user never learns a technical term, never sees a diagram, and can verify every step in eight seconds. That last part matters more than credibility — a scientist does not have to trust Parallax if they can check it.

LoopHow it feels

The machine works while you are gone

The product lives at two timescales that look opposed and are not. Ninety seconds between meetings, and three hours deep in a commissioned dossier. The short visit is only possible because of the long work that happened in the dark.

01

You ask

A question, in your words. Private by default — a search history reveals what you are working on, and no serious researcher will use a tool that leaks it.

02

It works for hours

The full perimeter, the whole history, the live data, the cross-checks. You are not waiting at a spinner. You have left.

03

You check in ninety seconds

Something moved under you. Someone took a position against you. Here is the cheapest thing you could measure this year.

04

You go deep when it is real

Three hours in the dossier, tracing numbers down to their origins, reading the 1962 paper nobody cites.

05

It becomes a Watch, and never closes

Six weeks later a paper lands and the dossier rewrites itself — and tells you which paragraph changed, and why.

Every other product trades your time for its content. This one trades its time for your attention.

Which produces the idea the whole company rests on. Everywhere else, a query is disposable — you search, you get results, it is over; you ask a chatbot, it answers, it is gone; a research report starts dying the morning after it is written. In Parallax, asking a question creates a permanent object with a name that keeps working forever. After two years a researcher has sixty living questions, and leaving means killing all sixty. That is retention that nobody has to be manipulated into.

RankingReach, not taste

Not "what will you read" — "what could you settle"

Ranking by popularity is fatal: the loudest claim wins, which in science means the wildest one, and in four months the frontier is a tabloid. Ranking by taste is the social-media failure — it converges on comfort, and a researcher who only sees their own subfield never gets the cross-field connection, which is where breakthroughs actually come from.

So the signal is reach: what are you, specifically, in a position to go and settle this year? Your instruments, your methods, your collaborators, your access. And the profile needs no cold start — connect an ORCID or an arXiv ID and your entire published record is the profile, richer than a month of watching would produce.

Lane 1

Your field

What you would have found in three months, today. This earns the daily open.

Lane 2

Your reach

Outside your field, but you hold the method or the instrument. This is where the value is.

Lane 3

Your blind spot

One thing, deliberately far away, no justification offered. You cannot push someone toward larger concepts from inside their own citation circle.

Anything that threatens your own published work is always ranked up. Never down. No matter what you click.

The feed may learn what interests you. It is never permitted to learn to protect you. Every other platform hides what upsets you; this one is required to lead with it — otherwise personalisation turns a research instrument into a confirmation machine, which would make scientists measurably worse at their jobs.

And every card carries one editable line: shown because you measured this in 2021. No social platform shows its reasons, because transparency costs engagement. Here it is how trust is earned — and it is the safeguard against Parallax quietly becoming the thing that decides what science people see.

PositionYou vs the field

What you think, against what the world believes

Parallax can read your position out of your published work — no survey, no setup. Then it can show you things no researcher has ever been able to see about themselves.

·

Where you are in the minority — and whether the evidence is with you

Being outnumbered and being wrong are different facts. Nobody separates them today.

·

Where you are alone

The best or the worst news of a career, and worth knowing either way.

·

Where you contradict yourself

Your 2019 position does not survive your own 2024 paper. Twenty years of drift is invisible from inside it.

·

Where you were right first

Timestamped, provable, and yours. Priority is the actual currency of science.

Aggregate every position and you get something that has never existed: a map of what a field actually believes, as distinct from what it publishes. Those two differ enormously, everyone knows it, and nobody has ever measured the gap.

With one guardrail — consensus is not truth, so belief is only ever shown against evidence. Which yields the single most provocative readout in the product:

The distance between how sure a field is and how strong its evidence actually is. That number is what questions people.

TrustWhy believe it

The machine has to be checkable, scored, and able to say it does not know

01

Receipts by default

No assertion without its trace attached. Verifiability beats credibility, and it is the only form of trust that survives a confident machine being wrong once.

02

A public scorecard, including the losses

Claims resolve — confirmed, refuted, still open — and the accuracy is on the site permanently. The first system in history willing to publish its own error rate. In two years that number cannot be faked or copied.

03

The right to say "I don't know"

A machine that always has an answer is a machine that is guessing. Returning the literature does not determine this is what gives the confident answers meaning.

04

Depth as evidence

It reaches to the 1600s and cites what it finds. Forgotten is not refuted — and being able to prove that distinction is the thing no competitor can shortcut, because it requires having read what nobody reads.

StatusBuilt vs next

What exists, and what the next step is

The record eye was already built: eighteen archives, a sharded perimeter across roughly 124 passes, the historical ladder that reserves one window per decade back past 1930 so relevance ranking never buries the founding work, a relevance gate that reads every abstract rather than matching keywords, and cross-index corroboration.

The second eye is now open. The mechanism runs end to end, deployed, on a schedule anyone can trigger:

✓

Four live observatories

The NASA Exoplanet Archive, the gravitational wave catalogue, JPL's small-body database and SIMBAD, each returning measured values with error bars and a provenance string, normalised to SI before anything is compared.

✓

Numbers pulled out of prose, with receipts

Papers are fetched for the objects in hand and ranked by whether they are about them. Every stated value must appear as digits in the text it was read from, must convert into the dimension its quantity declares, and must fall inside a magnitude that quantity can plausibly have.

✓

The comparison, as arithmetic

Sigma is the disagreement over the joint error bar. No model runs past that line. A reader who trusts none of it can recompute it by hand.

✓

Claims as permanent, citable objects

Deterministic identifiers of the form PARALLAX-2026-04417, so the same disagreement re-derived tomorrow is the same claim rather than a second one.

✓

An absence that has been searched for

Before saying nothing explains an object, it looks, and carries the hit count as its receipt. It fails closed: during a total outage of that index it published nothing at all rather than assert an absence it could not verify.

It has never published a claim it could not defend.

That is not a boast about accuracy, it is a description of the rules holding. It withdrew ten claims about asteroids when a search returned 910 papers on the first of them. It rewrote three that contradicted their own provenance. It retracted a plausible, field-relevant 3.5σ tension because the number behind it was not in the source text.

What remains:

01

The computed figure

Every claim already carries the data its figure is drawn from. What is missing is the renderer — one per claim type. This is the shareable artefact and it is what makes the frontier scrollable.

02

The frontier itself

Claims exist and are stored. Nobody can look at them yet.

03

Watch, and the diff

Questions that stay open, and the paragraph-level change notice when reality moves.

04

Resolution, and the scorecard in public

The table and the view exist. What is missing is the act of closing a claim — confirmed, refuted — which is what makes the published accuracy real rather than promised.

OpenUnresolved

Still undecided

·

Which domain takes the first frontier

A scheduling decision, not a vision decision. It wants dense quantitative dependencies, open full text, and a live stream.

·

What happens the first time it is spectacularly wrong

It will be. The scorecard is the answer, but the voice and the correction ritual need designing before it happens, not after.

·

Where the line sits on speculation

The kill condition is the rule. But the bulk, extra dimensions, white holes — these are exactly the questions worth putting on a frontier and exactly the ones that can cost the product its credibility. The rule needs testing against real cases.

·

How positions stay honest at scale

A public belief map is a new instrument and also a new social pressure. Making it visible may change what people are willing to say.
