What it does with what it finds

01

The 1930s don’t get buried

Rank by relevance and the oldest work falls off the end. Each index is searched once per decade instead, so the founding papers come back with the preprints.

02

Every abstract gets read

Not keyword-matched. Each one is scored against your question — what it bears on, which side it takes, whether it reports a number — and it tells you why.

03

The indices check each other

A paper five indices found on their own is a different thing from one only Zenodo has. You see which is which, and what you’d lose by dropping any of them.

04

The whole history, not the first page

When the field went quiet and what restarted it. Whether current papers still cite the ones they are built on. How few groups it actually rests on.

05

Two theories, side by side

What each one explains that the other cannot, what they are both on the hook for, and the measurement that would tell them apart.

06

Your draft, against the record

Paste it in. Every claim comes back supported, contested, unsupported, or already published by someone else — with what to cite and what is exposed.
